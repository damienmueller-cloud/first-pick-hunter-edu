Strata special levies & building defects for Newcastle unit sellers

Plain-English Hunter education for apartment and unit owners preparing to sell — how special levies differ from regular strata contributions, why buyers scrutinise defects, and what to gather before photography and opens. This is not legal advice and not a valuation. Confirm disclosure, contract wording and any dollar amounts with your solicitor or conveyancer and the agents you interview.

Special levy vs admin / capital works levies (plain English)

Most NSW strata schemes collect regular contributions into an administrative fund (day-to-day running) and a capital works fund (longer-term building maintenance and renewals). Labels on notices can vary; the idea is the same: owners pay planned shares into shared pots.

A special levy is an extra contribution the owners corporation may raise when those funds are not enough for a particular shortfall or project — for example urgent waterproofing, lift works, façade repair or an insurance excess after an event. Exact powers, notice periods and voting rules sit in NSW strata legislation and your scheme’s records. Do not rely on a webpage for legal thresholds; ask your solicitor.

  • Regular levies = ongoing planned contributions.
  • Special levy = additional call for a defined need (or shortfall).
  • Marketing copy should describe facts you can support with notices and minutes — never invent a dollar figure.

Why buyers care

Serious apartment buyers — owner-occupiers and investors — often read the strata pack as carefully as the floor plan. Qualitatively, they worry about:

  • Surprise bills after settlement if a special levy is already flagged or likely.
  • Unfinished common-property works that affect liveability, noise or access during their ownership.
  • Insurance and building reputation — whether claims history, defect notices or incomplete remediation will complicate finance, cover or future resale.

None of that requires inventing medians, clearance rates or “ROI”. It does require honest early framing so buyers are not blindsided mid-campaign. Building defects: early solicitor-led disclosure vs mid-campaign surprise

Known building defects, rectification programs or insurer correspondence belong in a disclosure plan agreed with your solicitor or conveyancer before listing. Holding material documents until after an offer often slows finance, invites renegotiation or damages trust at open homes.

  • Ask what must sit in the contract pack versus what can be summarised for opens.
  • Align agent talking points with solicitor wording — agents should not improvise legal conclusions.
  • A mid-campaign “we just found this report” moment usually hurts more than a calm, early summary with documents available to serious buyers.

What to gather before listing

Ask your solicitor and shortlisted agents which items they want ready before photography and the first open. Sellers commonly assemble:

  • Recent AGM / EGM minutes and any resolutions about works or levies.
  • Current and recent levy notices (admin, capital works, and any special levy).
  • The scheme’s capital works / sinking plan (or equivalent planning document).
  • Any known defect reports, engineer letters, builder warranties or insurance correspondence relevant to the common property or your lot.
  • Clarity on parking/storage titles and pets/short-stay by-laws if they affect buyer filters.

If something is missing, say so early — “we are obtaining X from the strata manager” is better than silence. Presentation still matters; see Preparing your home to sell. Investors should also read Investment property CGT questions with their accountant (tax is separate from strata disclosure).

How agents summarise strata at opens (without overselling)

At opens, a strong apartment agent summarises known facts in plain English, invites serious buyers to review the pack, and refuses to guess dollar outcomes or “guarantee” that a levy will not happen. Overselling (“the building is fine, don’t worry about strata”) is a trust risk when documents say otherwise.

  • Stick to documented facts and solicitor-approved phrasing.
  • Point buyers to building-matched comps, not house medians — see Understanding comparable sales in the Hunter.
  • Separate lot presentation from common-property condition: you can stage your unit; you cannot rewrite the foyer.

Campaign honesty when a special levy or known works exist

If a special levy is struck, proposed or clearly foreshadowed — or major works are underway — build that into the campaign plan. Honest framing attracts buyers who can live with the facts; surprise discovery mid-campaign often produces price chips or walk-aways.

  • Do not invent levy amounts, “typical” special-levy sizes, or suburb rankings on education pages or in ads.
  • Use the actual notices and minutes; let your solicitor confirm what must be stated in the contract.
  • Agree who answers buyer questions about works timing — agent, strata manager, or solicitor referral.

Appraisal questions for levies and defects

Take these when you interview agents (adapt for your building):

  • How will you summarise our levies, capital works plan and any special levy for open-home buyers?
  • What building-matched comps will you use, and how do those sales treat similar strata issues?
  • Have you recently listed or sold in this building or tightly comparable buildings?
  • What documents do you want in the pack before we go live?
  • If a defect or works program is known, how do you recommend we disclose without overselling?
  • Auction or private treaty for this unit type and buyer pool?
