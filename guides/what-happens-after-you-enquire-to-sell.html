What happens after you enquire to sell

You asked for an appraisal or agent contact. Here is a calm Hunter NSW checklist for what good follow-up looks like — and how to stay in control before you list.

What “good” follow-up usually looks like

After an enquiry, a reputable agency should acknowledge you, confirm your suburb and timeframe, then arrange a confidential appraisal conversation. Your details should not be auctioned to multiple offices without your consent. If the first reply is only a high stretch price with no comps, ask for evidence.

Prepare before the appraisal call or visit

  • Your rough timeframe and must-have outcomes (price certainty vs speed vs minimal disruption).
  • Known property facts: renovations, strata, easements, tenancy, or planned works.
  • Two or three nearby sales you have noticed — useful when comparing how agents use comps.
  • Questions on commission, marketing inclusions, who runs opens, and written feedback cadence.

Related: What to ask at a property appraisal · Comparable sales · How to choose a selling agent.

Questions that protect you

  • Which recent comparable sales support this range — and why do they apply to my home?
  • Who is my day-to-day contact if I list?
  • What is included in marketing vs optional extras?
  • Auction or private treaty for this property type and buyer pool — and why?
  • Am I under any obligation by requesting this appraisal? (You should not be.)

After the appraisal — still your decision

Take notes. Compare advice, not just headline dollars. If you proceed to list, settlement and deposits in NSW typically flow through solicitors/conveyancers and trust accounts — treat any pressure for card deposits on a marketing site as a red flag.
